Partner With Move Out Japan

Partnership enquiries open  |  Page updated 8 November 2026

This is an enquiry page, not an announcement. Move Out Japan is not publishing a partner list, a commission rate, or an “official partner” claim here. Any arrangement starts with a written agreement, and any paid placement is labelled where it appears. Read the Affiliate and Sponsorship Disclosure for the standing policy.

Move Out Japan is an English-language furniture disposal and move-out support service for foreign residents in Japan. We work with property managers, landlords, letting agents, relocation advisers, content creators and service providers who want to make leaving a Japanese apartment clearer and less stressful.

If you serve the same customer at a different point in the move, a partnership may make sense. This page explains who we want to hear from, the models we consider, what we assess, and how to start a conversation. For the longer version, read Partner With Move Out Japan: Referrals, Content and Service Collaborations.

Who we want to hear from

🏠 Property managers, landlords and agents

You handle the lease, inspection and handover. A reliable clearance option helps tenants leave the property in a usable condition and reduces abandoned items.

✈️ Relocation and expat services

You help people arrive, settle in or leave. Furniture disposal is often the last item on the checklist; a referral can give your client a clear next step.

📝 Content creators and communities

You publish practical guides or answer moving-out questions. A content collaboration may fit if the topic is useful and the claims can be sourced.

🔧 Service providers

Cleaning, storage, furniture, connectivity, housing, insurance or banking. If your service helps a foreign resident set up or move out, it may be relevant.

Ways to work together

1

Referral arrangement

You introduce a customer who needs a clearance; we quote and deliver. We agree in writing how referrals are tracked, what information is shared, and whether a fee applies. No volume or exclusivity promises.

2

Content collaboration

A guide, checklist or explainer that answers a real question. We edit for accuracy, cite primary sources where possible, and label paid or sponsored work.

3

Service collaboration

A clear sequence: what is included, who is responsible for each step, how the customer pays, and what happens if something goes wrong.

4

Affiliate or referral link

If we join a public programme that fits our audience, we may use a tracked link. We disclose it on the page and on the disclosure page. We do not publish rates without written approval.

What we assess

  • Relevance: does this help a foreign resident move out of, or settle into, a Japanese home?
  • Clear customer terms: scope, price or pricing method, cancellation, and a complaints route.
  • Data handling: what personal data changes hands, why, and how long it is kept. We do not sell leads.
  • Disclosure: willingness to be labelled as a partner, sponsor or affiliate where that is accurate.
  • Operational fit: coverage, language and capacity. We confirm coverage per job rather than claiming nationwide service.
  • Evidence: claims we can check. We do not publish invented testimonials, approvals or performance figures.

What we will not publish

  • invented partners, testimonials, approvals or business contacts;
  • commission rates or unapproved offers;
  • paid link insertions disguised as editorial;
  • guarantees about deposits, visas, prices or response times;
  • “leading”, “nationwide” or “official partner” claims without evidence.

What happens next

We review enquiries when we can. We do not promise a response time, and we do not guarantee that an enquiry will lead to a partnership. If the fit is not there, we will try to say so clearly rather than leave the conversation open.

If an idea looks promising, we will ask for the details we need to assess it: the exact scope, the customer journey, the disclosure wording, and the written terms. Nothing is published until both sides have agreed the text and the relationship is labelled accurately.

Disclosure and editorial independence

We follow the Consumer Affairs Agency’s stealth-marketing guidance: a commercial relationship should be clear and visible, not buried in small print. Affiliate links are disclosed on the page where they appear. Sponsored or paid content is labelled near the top. A fee does not buy a positive recommendation.
