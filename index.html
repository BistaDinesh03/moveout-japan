Common Problems Foreigners Face When Moving Out

Moving out in Japan gets stressful fast — especially when you don't understand the recycling system.

Worried About Losing Your Deposit

If appliances are left behind, landlords deduct expensive removal fees from your deposit (敷金). Some students lose ¥30,000–¥60,000.

Don't Understand Japanese Garbage Rules

City office websites, recycling forms, and phone calls are confusing. Sodai gomi (oversized garbage) rules vary by city.

Heavy Appliances & No Transportation

Fridges, washing machines, and TVs are heavy. You need help carrying them and a legal way to dispose of them under Japan's Home Appliance Recycling Law.

Frequently Asked Questions About Appliance Disposal in Japan

How do foreigners dispose of appliances in Japan?

Foreigners can dispose of appliances legally through a licensed disposal service like Move Out Japan. We handle all the paperwork and recycling requirements under Japan's Home Appliance Recycling Law, so you don't have to navigate the complex Japanese system alone.

What is sodai gomi and how does it work?

Sodai gomi (粗大ごみ) is the Japanese term for oversized garbage. Items like furniture, futons, and bicycles fall under this category. You usually need to buy a sticker from a convenience store and schedule a pickup. We simplify this process — we handle everything for you.

Can international students use this service?

Absolutely. Our service is specifically designed for foreign students and international residents in Japan. We provide full English support and flexible payment options that don't require a Japanese credit card.

How much does appliance disposal cost in Japan?

Our pricing starts at ¥8,000 for a washing machine and ¥10,000 for a refrigerator, including all recycling fees and pickup. The exact cost depends on the item type and size. We always provide a free, no-obligation quote before you commit.

Do I need to carry appliances outside myself?

No. We help carry heavy items like fridges and washing machines from inside your apartment. Our team handles all the heavy lifting.

Is the disposal really legal?

Yes, 100%. All appliances are disposed according to Japan's Home Appliance Recycling Law (家電リサイクル法) and local municipal regulations. You will not face any legal issues or deposit deductions.

What areas do you cover for move-out support?

We currently serve Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka, Nagoya, Kyoto, and Yokohama. If you're located near these cities, please contact us to confirm availability.
