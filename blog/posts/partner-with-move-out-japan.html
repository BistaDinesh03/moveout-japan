Partner With Move Out Japan: Referrals, Content and Service Collaborations

Published November 8, 2026  |  Partnerships  |  11 min read

Move Out Japan is a small, English-language move-out support service. We work with property managers, relocation advisers, content creators and service providers who want to make leaving a Japanese apartment less confusing for foreign residents. This guide explains how we approach partnerships, what we will and will not publish, and how to start a conversation. It is also a record of the public programme pages we reviewed while building our partnership policy — not a list of partners.

🤝 No partnership is announced on this page. This is an enquiry guide, not an offer. We do not publish commission rates, guarantees, partner logos, or “official partner” claims without a written agreement. Any future paid placement will be labelled clearly.

The Short Answer

We are open to partnership enquiries, but we are not a marketplace, a lead broker, or a self-serve affiliate programme. There is no published rate card. If a referral, content, service or affiliate arrangement makes sense, we agree the scope in writing for that specific partner and disclose any paid element where it appears.

We are deliberately cautious about what appears on this site. A partnership page is easy to fill with logos, percentages and claims that nobody has approved. We would rather publish less and be able to stand behind every sentence. That is also why this article cites public programme pages as research examples rather than presenting them as our partners.

If you want to start a conversation, email dineshbista123@outlook.com with “Move Out Japan - Partnership Enquiry” in the subject, or message us on LINE at @704xslsr. The partners page has the short version and a checklist of what to include.

1. Who This Is For

Our customers are usually foreign residents who are leaving an apartment in Japan and need furniture, appliances and bulky waste cleared, often while handling a lease handover at the same time. The people who already serve that customer are the most natural partners.

🏠 Property managers, landlords and letting agents

You handle the lease, the inspection and the handover. A reliable clearance option helps a tenant leave the property in a usable condition and reduces the pile of abandoned items that someone else has to deal with. We can discuss a referral arrangement or a repeat-service workflow.

✈️ Relocation and expat services

You help people arrive, settle in or leave. Furniture disposal is often the last item on a long checklist. A referral or service collaboration can give your client a clear next step without you having to manage the clearance yourself.

📝 Content creators and community organisers

If you publish practical guides, run a community group, or answer the same moving-out questions repeatedly, a content collaboration may fit. We care about accuracy, official sources and disclosures more than volume.

🔧 Service providers

Cleaning, storage, furniture, internet or SIM services, housing, insurance and banking all sit next to a move-out. If your service helps a foreign resident set up or leave, it may be relevant. Relevance is not the same as endorsement, and we assess fit case by case.

We are not a good fit for every enquiry. If your audience is outside Japan, your service is unrelated to moving out, or your proposed collaboration depends on claims we cannot verify, we will say so rather than publish something misleading.

2. Partnership Models We Consider

“Partnership” can mean very different things. We use the word only when the arrangement has been discussed and agreed. These are the models we are willing to explore; none is active merely because it is described here.

1

Referral arrangement

You introduce a customer who needs a clearance; we quote and deliver the job. We agree in writing how the referral is tracked, what information is shared, and whether any fee applies. We do not promise volume, exclusivity or a particular conversion rate.

2

Content collaboration

We work together on a guide, checklist or explainer that answers a real question for foreign residents. We edit for accuracy, link to primary sources where possible, and label paid or sponsored work. Editorial control stays with the site that publishes it.

3

Service collaboration

Your service and our clearance can be offered as a clear sequence: what is included, who is responsible for each step, how the customer pays, and what happens if something goes wrong. The customer should never have to guess which company is responsible for what.

4

Affiliate or referral link

If we join a public affiliate programme that fits our audience, we may use a tracked link. We disclose the link on the page and on the disclosure page. We do not reproduce commission rates without written approval.

📝 What “agreed in writing” means here: a short email or document that names the parties, the scope, the customer journey, any fee or tracked link, the disclosure wording, and the date it starts. If it is not written down, it is not a partnership we will advertise.

3. What We Assess Before Any Collaboration

We are a small operation, so the assessment is practical rather than bureaucratic. The questions below are the ones that decide whether an enquiry moves forward.

  • Relevance. Does this help a foreign resident move out of, or settle into, a Japanese home? A service can be adjacent to that journey without being useful to our readers.
  • Clear customer terms. Can a customer see the scope, the price or pricing method, cancellation rules, and the complaints route before they commit? We avoid arrangements that depend on hidden fees or pressure.
  • Data handling. What personal data would change hands, why, and how long is it kept? We do not sell leads or share quote-request data. If a customer asks for an introduction, we ask them first.
  • Disclosure. Is the partner willing to be labelled as a partner, sponsor or affiliate where that is accurate? A partner who wants to look independent when they are not is not a fit.
  • Operational fit. Can the service actually be delivered in the customer’s area, in a language the customer understands, at the time they need it? We confirm coverage per job rather than claiming nationwide service.
  • Evidence. Can the partner support factual claims with something we can check? We do not publish invented testimonials, approvals, awards or performance figures.

We also look at the public programme terms when an affiliate network is involved. Some programmes require a Japanese bank account, a Japanese contact address, or a Japanese-language site. Those requirements can rule out an otherwise interesting programme for an English-language site like this one. We would rather note the mismatch than pretend the programme is available.

5. What We Publish — and What We Won't

This is the part that matters most to us. A partnership can change what a reader sees, so the publishing rules have to be clear before any commercial conversation starts.

✅ We will

  • describe a partnership as active only after both sides have agreed;
  • label paid, sponsored or affiliate content where it appears;
  • link to public programme pages as research examples, with context;
  • correct factual errors and date substantive changes;
  • keep editorial recommendations independent of any fee.

❌ We won't

  • invent partners, testimonials, approvals or business contacts;
  • publish commission rates or unapproved offers;
  • sell link insertions or disguise paid links as editorial;
  • guarantee a deposit refund, visa outcome, price or response time;
  • share quote-request data with a partner without the customer’s knowledge.

We also avoid language we cannot support. “Leading”, “nationwide”, “guaranteed”, “official partner” and similar phrases are easy to write and hard to prove. If a partner wants that language in a placement, we will ask for the evidence; if there is none, the wording changes or the placement does not run.

6. Disclosure and Editorial Independence

Disclosure is not a footer afterthought. The Consumer Affairs Agency’s guidance on stealth marketing says an affiliate site should make its commercial relationship clear and visible, and that the wording at the top of the page must be clear in size and colour. The guidance also notes that a paid or requested post can amount to “business representation” and needs to be labelled accordingly. We treat those principles as the minimum.

In practice, that means:

  • an affiliate link is disclosed on the page where it appears, not only in a site-wide footer;
  • a sponsored article or paid placement carries a visible label near the top;
  • the label says what the relationship is, in plain language;
  • the disclosure page explains the policy across the site;
  • a paid placement does not buy a positive recommendation.

Our Affiliate and Sponsorship Disclosure page is the standing version of this policy. It explains how we handle affiliate links, sponsored content and editorial independence, and it links back to this guide and to our Privacy Notice.

⚖️ Editorial independence is the rule, not a slogan. If we cannot recommend a service on its merits, we decline the placement. A fee can support the work of publishing; it cannot buy the conclusion.

For the official guidance, see the Consumer Affairs Agency’s stealth-marketing Q&A (Japanese) and its English-language site. This article is a practical policy summary, not legal advice; if you need a legal opinion on your own disclosure, consult a qualified professional.

7. The Public Programme Pages We Reviewed

While building this policy, we looked at public partnership and affiliate pages from services that operate in the same broad space: housing, connectivity, insurance and money transfer. The purpose was to understand how published programmes describe eligibility, application and disclosure — not to sign up to all of them.

The pages below were checked on 8 November 2026. They are third-party pages, not Move Out Japan partnerships. We do not reproduce their commission rates, and we do not claim that we have joined or been approved by any of them. Programme terms change, so confirm the current details on the provider’s site.

  • Oakhouse — public affiliate page: a housing provider’s published programme page. The provider sets eligibility, application and payment terms.
  • Mobal — public affiliate programme: a voice, data and connectivity provider’s programme page, including application and approval information.
  • Japan Wireless — public affiliate page: a rental WiFi and SIM provider’s programme page, with an application that is reviewed by the provider.
  • Sakura Mobile — public affiliate programme: a connectivity provider’s programme page describing eligibility and an approval process.
  • SafetyWing — public ambassador programme: a travel and health insurance provider’s published ambassador page.
  • Wise — public partner page: a money-transfer provider’s published partnership page.
  • Awin — publisher network: a global affiliate network where each brand sets its own acceptance criteria.

What we took from that review is less about any single programme and more about the questions worth asking: Who approves the application? What does the programme require of the publisher? How is the link tracked? When and how is a relationship disclosed? What happens if the customer has a problem? Those questions apply whether the arrangement is a formal affiliate programme or a simple referral between two small businesses.

8. A Practical Checklist

If you are preparing an enquiry, this list will save a round of questions.

📋 Before you write

  • Define the customer problem your partnership would solve.
  • Write the scope in one paragraph: who does what, for whom, and when.
  • Confirm your price or pricing method and your cancellation rules.
  • Decide how customer data would be handled and stored.
  • Decide whether any paid, sponsored or tracked element is involved.

📄 Before anything is published

  • Put the agreement in writing, including the disclosure wording.
  • Approve the exact text and links that will appear.
  • Check every factual claim against a source you can show us.
  • Label the relationship where the reader will see it.
  • Date the page and agree who updates it if terms change.

If you are not sure whether your idea fits, send the short version first. A two-paragraph email is enough to start; we can ask for the rest if the fit looks promising.

If you are here as a customer rather than a partner, nothing changes about the service. Quotes are still based on what you need cleared, and you can request a free quote or read what the service covers. The pricing page explains what a quote depends on before you decide.

If we ever earn a fee from a link or a placement, we will disclose it on the page where it appears. We will not use a partnership to make a recommendation we would not otherwise make. And we will not share your quote-request details with a partner without asking you first.
